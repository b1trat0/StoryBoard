A strong spine with no skin on half of it.

The database, the IPC boundary and the export converter are built to a standard most side projects never reach. Four of the seven screens do not exist, there is no backup of the only copy of the manuscript, and nothing anywhere saves your typing when Windows closes the window. And despite the schema being generous, the app is currently hard-wired to one Royal Road serial — not a novel, not a research book.

What this is: a read-only audit of the code as it stands, not a plan review. Every number below was measured in the repo, not estimated.

The good news: the missing screens need no new backend. facts, relations, glossary and illustrations already have repositories, services, zod schemas and 34 wired IPC channels. What is missing is the renderer.

The bad news: the manuscript currently lives in exactly one Docker volume, and the command that was supposed to back it up (npm run db:dump) points at a file that does not exist.

Second bad news: there is exactly one project, its id is a constant in the source, and the schema cannot cite a source that is not a chapter — so a research book is not "just a setting change".

03Is this only for light novels? Mostly, yes.

The honest answer is that the data model generalises better than the product does. Tables called chapters, entities and facts would serve a plain novel or a nonfiction book without complaint. What stops you is the layer above them: one hardcoded project, a mandatory volume → arc → chapter ladder, and an export pipeline that knows only Royal Road.

What you want to writeWorks today?What stands in the way
Light novel / web serial for Royal Road native Only the missing screens. The model, the converter and the export steps were designed for exactly this.
A second light novel (next series) no ACTIVE_PROJECT_ID is a constant in App.tsx, the seed creates one project titled "My Serial", and no projects:* channel exists. A second work means editing source and reseeding.
Plain novel, not on Royal Road, no illustrations partly You can write it. But chapters.arc_id is NOT NULL, so every book needs at least one invented arc; and the release preview gates on Royal Road rules (500-character minimum, ~10,000-word ceiling, "not finished") which would flag a perfectly normal manuscript as a blocker.
Novel with research material partly There is nowhere to put the research. facts.source_chapter_id is NOT NULL, so a note can only be sourced to your own prose, never to a book, article, interview or URL. Glossary notes and entity backstory are prose blobs, not a citation system.
Light novel + research material partly Same wall as above, plus a second problem: mixing research into story canon means the facts tab fills with material that is not canon. You would start distrusting the one view that is supposed to be trustworthy.
A research book / nonfiction no Missing: citations and a bibliography, a source table, footnote/endnote handling, section headings inside a chapter, figure and table numbering, a table of contents in the export, and entity types like concept or topic. pov_entity_id, arcs and POV breaks are meaningless here.

Why the fact invariant is the real blocker

AGENTS.md is explicit: every fact requires source_chapter_id and source_quote, enforced by NOT NULL plus a CHECK, "not application validation alone". That rule is the reason the facts tab can be trusted and the reason an AI pass cannot poison canon. It is also precisely what makes external research impossible: research is sourced to a book, not to chapter 14 of your own manuscript.

So do not relax it

Loosening source_chapter_id to nullable "so research fits" would delete the one guarantee that makes the app worth building. If research matters to you, add a parallel concept — research_sources and research_notes with their own citation columns — and keep facts exactly as strict as it is. Two tables with two rules beat one table with a loophole.

Three ways to generalise — pick one deliberately

OptionWhat changesYou getCost
A. Stay a light-novel tool Nothing. Say so in the UI and in AGENTS.md. One serial, done properly, sooner. Zero. And it is a legitimate choice.
B. Add a proper project layer works row with kind and export_profile; projects:* IPC; a project picker; neutral copy. Facts unchanged. Several novels, series or drafts side by side, each with its own cast and canon. This is the highest value-per-line change on this page. ~2–3 days. One migration, five channels, one picker screen.
C. Full research/nonfiction model research_sources, research_notes, note↔chapter links, entity types concept/topic, optional arcs, section headings, generic export profile. A real research book, and research material that never contaminates canon. ~1–2 weeks, plus every export test needs a second profile.
Recommendation

Do B now, at the same time as the missing screens. Do C only if you are actually writing nonfiction this year. B is a migration, five channels and a picker — a couple of days, and nothing already built gets thrown away, because every existing row already carries project_id. C is a second product wearing this one's clothes, and it would tempt you to weaken the fact invariant, which would cost you the only feature that cannot be re-bought.

05UI / UX: what is good, what to change

Keep exactly as it is

  • Two typefaces with two jobs. Serif for your words, sans for chrome. It reads like a book, not a dashboard, and it is the single reason the app feels authored rather than generated.
  • Native <dialog>. Focus trapping and Escape come from the platform, so nothing can break on an edge case you did not think of.
  • Honest unsaved state. "unsaved" next to the word count and "Saved only when you press Save" is a promise the app keeps. Do not replace it with a fake "Saved ✓" that means "we tried".
  • The draft lives above the section switch. The comment explaining why is better than most production code. Do not move it back into the editor.
  • Disabled nav for unbuilt sections. Showing the shape of the app instead of hiding the gaps is a good call.

One design rule worth writing down

The release preview renders the outgoing HTML rather than re-deriving it. Keep that. The moment a second renderer exists, there are two truths and only one of them gets published.

06Is Docker + Postgres too much? No — but it is a tax you should name.

For this project Postgres is doing real work that an embedded database would make you rebuild in application code: named enums, text[] aliases, jsonb metadata, partial indexes for unuploaded images, CHECK constraints that encode story rules, and pg_dump as a backup primitive you already trust. The schema is the best-engineered part of the repo, and it is good because it is Postgres.

OptionWhat you gainWhat you loseVerdict
Postgres in Docker
current		 Everything already built. Real constraints, real dumps, room for pgvector if the AI layer ever needs embeddings. Must start Docker before writing. Packaging cannot ship it, so a fresh machine needs three setup steps. If the volume is deleted, the book is gone. Keep for now.
Embedded Postgres
(PGlite / portable binaries)		 Schema unchanged — same SQL, same enums. The app becomes double-click-and-write, which is what a single-user desktop tool should be. Another runtime to vendor and update; pg_dump workflow changes; Drizzle's driver path differs. Best long-term answer, wrong time. Revisit after v1.
SQLite Zero install, one file, trivially backed up by copying it. No text[], no enums, no real CHECK-heavy modeling without rewriting migrations; loses the schema design you already invested in. No. You would be trading your best asset for convenience.
The actual problem is not Docker

It is that Docker is doing the job of a storage engine while also being the only copy of the manuscript. Docker is not too much. A single un-backed-up volume is too little. Fix the dump, keep two rotating copies outside the repo, and Docker stops being a risk — at which point the only honest complaint left is "I have to start it before I write".

Cheap operational wins

  • Nightly dump to a second drive via Task Scheduler, retention 14 days, filename includes a timestamp. Verify one restore by hand once, or the backup is a rumour.
  • Use the markdown+JSON archive as the second, human-readable backup. A .sql file is not a manuscript you can read in twenty years; .md is.
  • Copy illustrations/ too. A dump restores paths to image files that only exist on one disk.

07The DeepSeek layer

You are right that this is the remaining part. The schema was built for it and then left empty on purpose. The one rule that makes it safe is already enforceable in SQL: every fact carries a source chapter and a verbatim quote, and a model's output enters the table as status='draft'. The author accepts; the model never decides.

Three features, in the order they pay off

#FeatureReadsWritesWhy this order
1 English checker
grammar, tense drift, POV slips, glossary violations		 One chapter's body_md, the glossary terms Nothing. Returns a list of suggestions with character spans; you accept one at a time. Immediate value, lowest risk, and it validates your prompt and provider plumbing on text you can judge yourself.
2 Fact proposals
"what changed in this chapter"		 Chapter text + existing entities/aliases facts rows with status='draft', source_quote required This is the app's core promise. A rejected proposal is a useful signal; an auto-approved one poisons canon.
3 Continuity checks
contradictions, dropped threads, unused abilities		 Approved facts, relations, abilities, current chapter Nothing canonical — surfaces warnings in the release preview Needs #2 to exist first, because it reasons over approved facts rather than raw prose.

Where the code goes

renderer src/features/assist/ suggestion list · accept / reject · nothing else │ typed IPC (zod in and out, same as everything else) ▼ main ipc/handlers.ts ai:checkChapter · ai:proposeFacts services/assist.service.ts prompt assembly · span mapping · validation interfaces/ai-provider.interface.ts the only file that knows DeepSeek exists interfaces/deepseek.interface.ts fetch, timeout, retry, JSON mode ▼ DeepSeek api.deepseek.com · key from OS keychain, never from a file in the repo
  • The provider is an interface, not a client. One file imports fetch and holds the URL and model id. Swapping to a local model later is a new implementation, not a refactor.
  • Prompts live next to the service that uses them, versioned in git, with a comment saying what was verified against what. Not a system-prompt editor — AGENTS.md is right that a prompt editor is speculative until there are chapters.
  • Return JSON, validate with zod, reject on shape mismatch. A model that returns prose instead of JSON must fail loudly at the boundary, not half-parse into a fact.
  • Character spans, not rewritten prose. The checker highlights; you edit. The model never returns a paragraph for you to paste — that would make the AI an author.
  • Feed the glossary as a never-touch list. canonical_form plus aliases go in the prompt as terms that must survive untouched. This is the reason the glossary table exists.
  • Never send a whole volume. One chapter, or one chapter plus its approved facts. Context cost stays predictable and the model stops hallucinating across 200 chapters it half-remembers.
  • Log the request shape, the latency and the token counts, never the prose, in the existing logger. You will want to know which call was slow; you will not want a log full of your manuscript.

The graph, without a graph library

Nodes are entities; edges are relations. Filter both to the chapter you are standing on and you have "who is alive in this scene and how they feel about each other" — which is the question you actually ask while writing. Sentiment maps to edge colour, is_secret to a dashed stroke, relation_known_by to which nodes can "see" the edge.

A force layout is a dependency. A deterministic radial layout around the POV character is not, and it is easier to read: POV in the centre, factions as wedges, places on the rim. Ship that first; add physics only if it actually helps you think.

Two guardrails worth writing into AGENTS.md before the first line of AI code

1. No accepted suggestion may write prose into body_md. Ever. The checker returns spans, the author types.
2. No AI-created row may skip source_chapter_id and source_quote. The database already refuses it; the service must refuse it earlier so the error names the chapter, not the constraint.

Cost sanity

A chapter is a few thousand tokens in and a few hundred out, so a full pass over the story is small change at DeepSeek's current rates. The thing to design for is not cost, it is latency inside a flow that happens hundreds of times: stream or return a progress state, never freeze the editor waiting for a response, and never block saving on the model.

Each one adds a second source of truth or a second author. Your AGENTS.md already says this; the audit agrees with it.

09If you do only five things

  1. Write scripts/pg-dump.ts and schedule it. One day of work between you and losing the book. Everything else on this page is optional by comparison.
  2. Guard unsaved prose on quit, and add Ctrl+S. Two small changes that make the editor trustworthy. A writing tool that loses sentences gets abandoned, no matter how good its data model is.
  3. Build the Relations screen. It is the fastest path to the app earning its keep: the backend is finished, it is one screen, and "who knows what about whom" is the question a long serial actually asks.
  4. Build the Facts tab under the editor. Same reason: 100% of the IPC already exists. This is the tab that stops you rereading 200 pages.
  5. Then, and only then, the DeepSeek English checker. Do it as proposal-only, with the glossary as a never-touch list, and let it prove itself on chapters you have already written before it touches a chapter you are writing.
If the answer to "can I write something else" matters to you

Then fold option B (a real project layer, section 03) into step 3, not later. Every table already carries project_id; the hardcoded ACTIVE_PROJECT_ID is the only thing standing between the app and being able to hold a second work. Doing it while you are already touching the outline and the entity screens is a day of extra work — doing it afterwards means reopening all of them.

One sentence you can hold on to

You have built the hard half to a standard that does not need revisiting, and left the half the author actually touches unfinished. The next two weeks are not about design — they are about screens that consume an API that already works.
